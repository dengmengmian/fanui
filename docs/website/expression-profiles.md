# Marketing and Editorial Expression Profiles

Expression profiles make high-amplitude surfaces intentional without turning taste preferences into universal bans. They supplement experience calibration; they never override task clarity, content truth, accessibility, readability, or product evidence.

## Profile dimensions

Choose a bounded level for each dimension:

```yaml
expression_axes:
  composition_order: linear | layered | asymmetric
  visual_amplitude: low | medium | high
  motion_intensity: none | restrained | expressive
  narrative_axis: product_evidence | transformation | idea | people | craft
  recurring_motif: ...
  second_read_moment: ...
  image_strategy: supplied | documentary | product_derived | illustrative | typographic | none
  contrast_strategy: tonal | chromatic | spatial | typographic
  mobile_translation: ...
```

The profile is a brief, not a token set. It should explain the intended effect and the constraints that keep it coherent.

`composition_order` describes spatial organization only. Editorial subject matter belongs in `narrative_axis`; movement belongs in `motion_intensity`, so the dimensions do not overlap.

## Marketing guidance

Marketing surfaces may use higher contrast, larger scale, more spatial variation, and stronger transitions than Product UI. Select one primary narrative axis. Product-led claims should normally be supported by readable, high-fidelity product evidence rather than decorative application chrome.

Useful second-read moments include a meaningful state transition, a visual explanation of system behavior, or a detail that rewards closer inspection. A hidden joke or ornamental flourish is optional and cannot carry essential meaning.

Motion should clarify sequence, causality, spatial continuity, or product behavior. Respect reduced motion and preserve comprehension when animation is absent.

## Editorial guidance

Editorial expression begins with an idea in the article, not a generic cover style. Choose an image strategy that communicates the subject through artifacts, diagrams, traces, people, place, material, or a deliberate typographic concept.

The recurring motif may connect an issue or series, but should not make unrelated stories visually interchangeable. Large cover areas must earn their height with information or meaning. Article bodies remain optimized for sustained reading.

## Anti-default check

Before approval, ask:

- Could this composition be swapped into an unrelated AI/SaaS product without changing anything meaningful?
- Does the visual contain an idea or merely occupy area?
- Is variation supporting the narrative, or is every section using the same volume and card rhythm?
- Does Mobile preserve the idea, or only a cropped decoration?
- Are supplied brand assets, photography, and product evidence being used before generating substitutes?

There is no global ban on a typeface, centered Hero, grid, dark background, or particular color family. Reject choices because they conflict with the brief, evidence, or hierarchy—not because they appear on a style blacklist.
