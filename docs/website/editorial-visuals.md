# FanUI Editorial Visuals

## Purpose

Blog and editorial visuals should support ideas, not behave like empty decorative placeholders.

## Prohibited default

Avoid the common AI-generated cover:

```text
soft purple/blue gradient rectangle
└── large empty white rounded rectangle
```

It reads as a skeleton, not editorial art.

## Preferred visual sources

Use product/domain-derived compositions such as:

- workflow topology;
- run trace;
- approval state transition;
- artifact/version composition;
- code/data excerpt;
- system boundary diagram;
- agent/tool topology;
- timeline or evidence chain;
- abstracted product UI fragment when directly relevant.

The visual may be simplified, but it should contain an idea.

## Blog Index

Featured visuals may be larger and more branded. Latest-card visuals should vary by article concept rather than repeating the same gradient tile.

A good index visual system has family resemblance without identical composition.

## Blog Article Hero

A large Hero image is optional.

Choose one of three modes:

### Strong visual

Use approximately 820–980px wide and 360–520px tall when the visual carries real editorial information.

### Supporting visual

Use approximately 680–860px wide and 260–380px tall.

### No Hero visual

Start the article body after the header when the article is primarily textual and no useful visual exists.

Do not use a huge empty image only because the template has a Hero slot.

## Reading priority

Editorial visuals should not delay the start of the article excessively. At 1000px viewport height, the reader should normally encounter either the first paragraph or a meaningful visual plus the beginning of text in the first viewport.

## Brand expression

Editorial visuals may borrow brand geometry, tints, and typography, but should remain calmer than Homepage Hero composition.

## Hard rule

> Editorial area must be earned by editorial information.
